Land subsidence rate (millimetres/year), a global map for the historical period at a spatial resolution of 30 × 30 arc seconds (approx. 1 km at the equator), estimated with a deep learning model trained on observed subsidence and environmental covariates (see [paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL104497)).

Land subsidence -- gradual settlement or rapid sinking of the ground -- can occur as a result of natural factors (e.g., volcanic or seismic activity, collapse of subsurface cavities, compaction of loose fine-grained deposits) or anthropogenic activities (e.g., excessive groundwater abstraction, mining, subsurface energy extraction).

Davydzenka, T., Tahmasebi, P., & Shokri, N. (2023). [Data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10223637) for "Unveiling the Global Extent of Land Subsidence: The sinking crisis".
